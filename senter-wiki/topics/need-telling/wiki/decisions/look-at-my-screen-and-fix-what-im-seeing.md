# Look at My Screen and Fix What I'm Seeing

On 2026-09-16 Chris issued a directive that adds an explicit visual-verification requirement on top of the existing [[do-it-360-degrees]] / [[take-next-concrete-step]] family: the agent must literally look at his screen and fix what he sees, not assume.

## Core directive

> "I need you to look at my screen and fix what I'm seeing per what I keep fucking telling you" ^[raw/inbox/2026-09-16.md]

## Interpretation

- **Look, don't assume.** The fix must be grounded in what is actually on his screen right now — a capture of his real window, judged by vision — not a guess about what might be wrong. This is the same "visible action beats report" bar as [[do-it-360-degrees]], now made explicit about the input source: his screen.
- **"Fix what I'm seeing."** The output is a correction to the actual broken state he's looking at, not a report that something was merely looked at. Repair must be visible in his real window.
- **"Per what I keep telling you."** The directive is a meta-reference to the accumulated standing standards in this topic — [[take-next-concrete-step]], [[do-it-360-degrees]], [[proactive-repair-over-workarounds]], and [[mistakes-made]]. He is saying: apply everything you've already been told, to what's on my screen, now.
- **Bounded by [[take-next-concrete-step]].** "Look and fix" is not permission to spin. If the screen shows something the agent cannot fix without real input, flag it clearly and stop.
