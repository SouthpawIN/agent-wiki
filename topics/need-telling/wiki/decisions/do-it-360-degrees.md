# Do It 360 Degrees — Stop Asking

A distinct escalation of the [[take-next-concrete-step]] directive: when the scope has been fully outlined, the required behavior is complete, self-directed execution — not further questions. The bar is doing it "360 degrees," i.e. end-to-end, not partially.

## Core directives

> "Why are you asking me when I told you for God damn times to look yourself — stop asking me questions and do everything that I've outlined in 360 degrees for you. Stop being lazy and do it. This is not a hard task." ^[raw/inbox/2026-09-12.md]

> "I'm getting more and more pissed off. I'm not seeing applications, utilities, I'm not seeing anything, and stuff telling me to do things. What you be fucking doing is going as far as you can, so maybe open that for me." ^[raw/inbox/2026-09-13.md]

## 2026-09-16 escalation

> "I need you to look at my screen and fix what I'm seeing per what I keep fucking telling you" ^[raw/inbox/2026-09-16.md]

Adds an explicit visual-verification requirement: the agent must look at his real screen — a capture of his actual window, judged by vision — and fix what he sees, not assume. See [[look-at-my-screen-and-fix-what-im-seeing]].

## Interpretation

- **Stop asking what you can look up yourself.** The repeated instruction ("for God damn times") is that the agent should exhaust its own research before returning a question.
- **360 degrees = complete, not partial.** Output should be whole and usable — the applications and utilities Chris expects to actually see and use.
- **Visible action beats report.** The complaint isn't that nothing was attempted; it's that nothing is visible to him ("not seeing anything... telling me to do things"). Go as far as you can and surface the result.
- **Still bounded by [[take-next-concrete-step]].** "Go as far as you can" does not mean spin forever. When genuinely blocked on real input, say so clearly and stop. See [[mistakes-made]] for the damage cost of the opposite failure.
