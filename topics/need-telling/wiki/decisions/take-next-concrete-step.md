# Take the Next Concrete Step

An execution-discipline directive from Chris: agents working on recovery must advance the actual goal in discrete steps and explicitly signal terminal state — completion or blockage — rather than spinning or deferring indefinitely.

## Core directive

> "Take the next concrete step. If you believe the goal is complete, state so explicitly and stop. If you are blocked and need input from the user, say so clearly and stop." ^[raw/inbox/2026-09-08.md]

## Permission to act

On 2026-09-09, Chris added explicit approval for reversible action:

> "Undertand that we can undo choices you make easily, so do not be afraid to make them. Continue working toward this goal. Take the next concrete step." ^[raw/inbox/2026-09-09.md]

The core wording was filed repeatedly by the watcher — originating from message `20260903_001447_7bc70a`, first captured in `raw/inbox/2026-09-06.md`, and re-filed across three days: 3 times on 2026-09-08 (sim 0.08, 0.08, 0.22), 5 times on 2026-09-09 at 13:32 (sim 0.23) and 20:02–20:07 (sim 0.22 each), and the reversibility variant 4 times on 2026-09-09 at 20:05–20:07 (sim 0.08, 0.20, 0.20, 0.20). All copies of the core directive are identical; the reversibility quote is the only variant and adds undo-permission as a guard against hesitation.

## Interpretation

- Progress is measured in concrete steps toward the stated goal, not in effort expended.
- Completion is an explicit claim, not an assumption; the agent must state it and stop.
- Blockage is a flag for user input — not an excuse, not a workaround, not a declaration of impossibility. This directly echoes the rejection in [[fixing-what-you-fucked-up]]: "telling me that you can't reconstruct it is not an answer."
- Reversible choices are permitted; hesitation out of fear of permanence is not a valid reason to pause.
- Combined with [[pre-planning-and-recovery]], the directive implies: when a step hits a wall, the next action is to backtrack and be more granular on previous steps, not to halt or hand-wave.
