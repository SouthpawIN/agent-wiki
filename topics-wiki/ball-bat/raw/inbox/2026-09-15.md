## 2026-09-15 13:06 · hermes · 20260915_130613_222ebe · sim 0.73
The question has a blank in it — the classic version reads: "A bat and a ball cost $1.10. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost?" Assuming those are the intended numbers:

Let x = cost of the ball. Then the bat costs x + 1.00.

x + (x + 1.00) = 1.10
2x + 1.00 = 1.10
2x = 0.10
x = 0.05

The ball costs 5 cents (the bat $1.05). The trap answer is "10 cents" — that only works if the bat cost 90 cents, which contradicts "a dollar more." The point of the puzzle is that people subtract 1.10 − 1.00 = 0.10 and stop there. If the missing number wasn't $1.00, tell me and I'll redo it — the formula is always ball = (total − difference) / 2.
